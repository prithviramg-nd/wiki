## Comparison

| | BEI questions | Traditional questions |
|---|---|---|
| Core question | "What **did** you do?" | "What **would** you do?" |
| Basis | Real-life examples | Hypothetical answers |
| Grounding | Evidence-based | Opinion-based |
| Predictive of performance | More predictive | Less predictive |
| Probing | Deep probing of events | Limited probing |

## Why BEI wins

A hypothetical answer tells you what a candidate *thinks* they'd do — which tends to be the idealized, socially-desirable answer. An actual past example is evidence: the real decision, the real trade-off, the real outcome, and it can be probed far more deeply (what led to it, what they tried, what happened next) — all of which makes it a better predictor of how they'll actually perform.

## How BEI connects to the other notes

- Use the [[interviewing/question-techniques|funnel approach]] to get there: open with a BEI-style open question ("Tell me about a time..."), then probe ("What did you do next? What was the outcome?").
- Use [[interviewing/ksa-model|the KSA model]] to decide *what* real-life example to ask for — a BEI question targeting Knowledge, Skills, or Attitude derived from the JD.
- Structure/listen to the answer using [[interviewing/star-model|the STAR model]] (Situation, Task, Action, Result).
